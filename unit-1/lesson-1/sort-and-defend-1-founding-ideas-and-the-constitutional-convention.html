Too Weak? How Was It Fixed?

Founding Ideas and the Constitutional Convention · Sort the Events
BRIDGE QUESTION:Why was the first national government too weak, and what compromises fixed it?

Your job: sort 8 events, defend each choice with a fact, then answer the bridge question.

How to play

  1. Read the event card. Use Word help if a word is hard.
  2. Tap ★ A PROBLEM or ◆ A FIX.
  3. Defend it: write one fact from the card that proves your choice.
  4. After all 8 cards, write your answer to the bridge question.

1781–83Event card 1 of 8

One state says no to a tax

Congress asked to collect a small tax on imported goods (the Impost). Twelve states said yes. One state said no, first Rhode Island, then New York. The plan died.

Word help: Impost = a tax on goods brought in from other countries · veto = say no and block it · dissenting = disagreeing

Is this a problem or a fix?

1784Event card 2 of 8

Spain closes the Mississippi

Spain closed the lower Mississippi River to American boats. Farmers in the West needed the river to sell their crops. The weak national government had no strong way to answer.

Word help: lower Mississippi = the southern part of the river · navigation = travel by boat · lifeline = the one thing you need to live or earn money

Is this a problem or a fix?

1780sEvent card 3 of 8

States tax each other

New York taxed firewood from Connecticut and cabbages from New Jersey. New Jersey answered by taxing a lighthouse. Congress could not control trade between the states.

Word help: taxed = charged a fee · lighthouse = a tower with a light that guides ships · trade = buying and selling

Is this a problem or a fix?

1786–87Event card 4 of 8

Shays’ Rebellion

Farmers in western Massachusetts, deep in debt, closed the courts with weapons. Congress tried to raise troops but had no money to pay them. A private army paid by rich Bostonians stopped the rebels.

Word help: rebellion = a fight against the government · debt = money you owe · troops = soldiers · private = not part of the government

Is this a problem or a fix?

1787Event card 5 of 8

The Great Compromise

Large and small states were stuck. The deal: a House of Representatives based on population, and a Senate with two senators for every state.

Word help: compromise = each side gives up something · population = number of people · senator = a member of the Senate

Is this a problem or a fix?

1787Event card 6 of 8

The Three-Fifths Compromise

North and South argued about counting enslaved people for seats in the House. The deal counted three-fifths of enslaved people for seats and for taxes. It gave the South more power and kept slavery in the Constitution.

Word help: enslaved = forced to work without freedom or pay · representation = having seats in government · taxes = money paid to the government

Is this a problem or a fix?

1787Event card 7 of 8

One President, chosen by electors

Under the Articles there was no president to carry out the laws. The Convention created one President. State electors, not Congress, would choose that person.

Word help: executive = the branch that carries out the laws · electors = people chosen to vote for the President · Congress = the lawmakers

Is this a problem or a fix?

1788Event card 8 of 8

A Bill of Rights is promised

Anti-Federalists feared the new government could take away freedoms. To win their votes, Federalists promised to add a Bill of Rights. It was added in 1791.

Word help: Anti-Federalists = people who opposed the Constitution · Federalists = people who backed it · Bill of Rights = a list of freedoms the government cannot take away

Is this a problem or a fix?
