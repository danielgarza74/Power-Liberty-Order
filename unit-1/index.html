Unit overview

Unit 1 asks where American ideas about government came from and how they became a working constitution. You will follow the influence of Enlightenment philosophy and colonial experience, see why the first national government proved ineffective, and study how the principles of separation of powers and federalism work against the concentration of power.

  • HSS-PoAD.12.1.1Analyze the influence of ancient Greek, Roman, English, and leading European political thinkers such as John Locke, Charles-Louis Montesquieu, Niccolò Machiavelli, and William Blackstone on the development of American government.
  • HSS-PoAD.12.1.3Explain how the U.S. Constitution reflects a balance between the classical republican concern with promotion of the public good and the classical liberal concern with protecting individual rights; and discuss how the basic premises of liberal constitutionalism and democracy are joined in the Declaration of Independence as “self-evident truths.”
  • HSS-PoAD.12.1.4Explain how the Founding Fathers’ realistic view of human nature led directly to the establishment of a constitutional system that limited the power of the governors and the governed as articulated in the Federalist Papers.
